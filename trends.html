A change shown here is not a declared corporate action. These figures are derived from observed movement in published data, not from issuer announcements. A fund appearing under "Decreases" has not necessarily declared a distribution cut, and one under "Increases" has not necessarily declared a raise. Distribution amounts on option-income and single-stock funds fluctuate every period by design. For the official record on any fund, consult the issuer's own announcements and the fund's 19a-1 notices.

Distribution rate is not yield, and neither is total return. A distribution rate is the most recent payment annualized as if it repeated unchanged for a full year. It is not a forecast, not guaranteed, and not a measure of profit. A fund can pay a very high distribution while its share price declines, leaving an investor with a negative total return. Distributions may include ordinary income, capital gains, and return of capital; return of capital reduces your cost basis and can create tax consequences later. Yield alone tells you very little about whether a fund is making money.

How these numbers are calculated. We snapshot the PRO dataset daily and record a new data point only when a value actually changes. To compare across a window, the last known value is carried forward across days with no recorded change, and the current value is compared against the value in effect at the start of the window. Yield changes are expressed as a relative percentage, because a distribution moving from 50% to 30% is conventionally described as a 40% reduction. Total return changes are expressed in percentage points, because total return is already a percentage and a relative change of a percentage would be misleading.

Windows may be shorter than they appear. Where the selected window is longer than the history we have collected, the comparison runs from the earliest snapshot available and a notice is displayed above. Funds added to the dataset recently are excluded from the rankings and listed separately until they have enough history to measure. Moves smaller than the stated threshold are treated as unchanged to reduce noise.

Data may be delayed, incomplete, or wrong. Figures are compiled from public sources and are not real-time. Snapshots can be missed, source data can contain errors, and fund information can change without being reflected here immediately.
